# Playtest Notes

## First Version Checklist

- [ ] The full loop from Waiting Room to floor and back plays without crashes.
- [ ] Pension and upgrades survive quitting and reopening the game.
- [ ] A new player usually dies before beating the floor boss.
- [ ] A few rounds of upgrades make a win realistic.
- [ ] A normal run takes about 10 to 15 minutes.
- [ ] The cane combat feels good.

## Session Notes

- Round 1: item naming and moving between rooms feel good. Side room return door moved behind the player. Charge moved to holding attack, and right click now throws the cane. More content is planned.
- Round 2: cane throw and tap-and-hold charge feel better. Fixed a bug where the thrown cane stayed spinning instead of dropping.
- Round 3: added an optometrist selling glasses that show enemy health, and a large boss health bar.
- Round 4: added hit flash, hit stop, screen shake, death bursts, clearer attack warnings, player hurt feedback, flying coins and placeholder sounds.
- Round 5: sounds are fine for now and may change later. Fixed swings passing through enemies: swings and the thrown cane now test the enemy's whole body, a swing stays active while its arc shows, and the arc stays in the direction it was swung.
- Round 6: first real art. Grandpa's idle, walk and swing come from PixelLab. Still to judge in play: the swing's look, and his size next to placeholder enemies. Known gap: the sprite keeps holding the cane while the thrown cane is away.
- Round 7: added a mini map, a big map on M, and return doors. Only visited rooms show. Choosing a route seals the other one, and rooms remember pickups left behind.
- Round 8: Grandpa's collision box moved to his feet. Added Permission Slips, Overheated Thermoses, breakable blocks, loot pockets, locked chests and a locked treasure room. Next rounds: a shop paid in Pension, secret rooms, then special items with mana. Still to judge: drop rates, Thermos strength, and whether rewards make runs too easy.
- Round 9: Slips and Thermoses got their own always-visible HUD counters with icons, and each stacks to 99.
- Round 10: added a shop on every floor and the Retirement Plan. To decide: the real reward list for each tier, and whether a tier should pay once ever. Still to judge: prices, and whether paying in feels worth it.
- Round 11: income was too high. Halved every Pension source, about 124 on a full route instead of 249, so every upgrade takes about 10 clean runs instead of 5. Summons now pay nothing, because a mini boss left alive could be farmed forever. Earning more should come from paid upgrades.
- Next build, not yet run or played: secret rooms, four new combat rooms, a Keys board for rebinding, more Bribed Clerk levels, recipes crafted by Agnes, a crosshair instead of the aim dot, and fading copies of Grandpa behind a dodge.
- Living floor stage 1, checked headless only, not yet seen or played: floors are now rooms on a grid joined by side doors, and everything can be explored and walked back through. Rooms come in small, medium and large, with the camera following in large ones. Both maps are floor plans. - Living floor stage 2, checked headless and by looking at screenshots of several rooms, not yet played: rooms now hold rocks (bombs and boss attacks break them, the cane does not), barrels (cane, thrown cane, bombs and bosses break them; most hold nothing or a coin), holes (Grandpa and walking enemies cannot cross, shots, the thrown cane and ghosts can), bone piles, cuttable grass, cobwebs, puddles, floor cracks, torches and wall lanterns. Every room also gets a light scatter from its own seed, and the floor has patches of tone instead of a checkerboard. Both bosses break rocks, barrels and crates their sweep reaches. Still to judge in play: whether the fill light makes rooms too flat, how often barrels and rocks block a fight in a way that feels unfair, whether holes read clearly at speed, the coin and potion drop rates, and the placeholder drawings. Small potions heal one pill for now, and the later potion slot should take them instead.
- Living floor stage 3, checked with tests, a simulated player and screenshots in real windows, not yet played by a person: the floor is much harder. Regional Reaper 1200 health, Senior Adjuster 500, regular enemies about 50% tougher and about 50% faster with shorter windups and gaps. Four new enemies: Seasonal Temps (groups of 4 that surround), Notary Publics (aimed bursts, back away), Burnt-Out Auditors (rush in, 1 second fuse, blast hurts other enemies and breaks props) and Claims Denial Officers (slow, frontal shield, the thrown cane goes over it). Rooms hold about half again as many enemy cells, large ones most, and later main path rooms add up to two. The Reaper dashes through props, sweeps, fires rings and summons on marked spots, and runs attacks together below half health. The Adjuster sweeps, charges along a lane and summons across Grandpa's path. Bosses only start attacks from inside the view and hurry over when far away, so nothing starts off screen in large rooms. Enemies hold still for 0.6 seconds as a room starts. Income is back near the old target: a full clear paid about 330 before this stage, 514 with every rock and crate blasted, and now pays about 125, or about 140 with them. A coin from a prop is now worth 1, not a treasure's value. Estimates from a simple simulated player in real scenes, 30 runs each, who changes rooms directly and never uses the Thermos or the thrown cane. A good reflex player wins 4 of 30 on a fresh save, reaching the boss in 7, wins 11 of 30 with one level of pills, knees and cane, and 22 of 30 nearly maxed. On the old numbers the same player won 14 of 20 fresh and 20 of 20 maxed. A slower player wins 0 of 30 fresh and only 1 of 30 nearly maxed, mostly stopped by the Adjuster, which may mean the slow end is too hard, so watch that first. Boss fight time is about 28 to 40 seconds and the Adjuster about 11 to 13, against about 10 and 5 before. Still to judge in play: whether the target really holds for a person, whether the Adjuster is a wall, whether warnings read in a crowd, whether a shop item at 60 now costs too much of a run's income, whether the slower income makes upgrades feel slow, how long a run takes, the block sound, and every placeholder drawing.

- Living floor stage 5, checked with tests, the full-loop check and screenshots in real windows, not yet played by a person: every run starts in the Crossroads, a small room with two signposted exits. West is the Records Basement, the old floor under a new name, one skull and always open. East is the Unkempt Cemetery, two skulls, chained shut with "Beat a floor boss first" until a floor boss of either path has fallen. Both maps name the floor, and the Crossroads stays on them as a small linked room. The Cemetery is outdoors: mossy greens and greys, cold fog-colored light, gravestones and dead trees as solid props, stumps, moss, saplings and more grass as decor, and it favors the newer enemies. New enemy: the Delinquent Plot Holder, 46 health, speed 58, which tunnels as a mound nothing can hit and rises under Grandpa after a warning of 0.85 seconds, then stands in the open for 2.8. New mini boss: Gus, Head Groundskeeper, 500 health, with a slam, a furrow along a line and clippings around Grandpa. New floor boss: Mortimer, Tired Undertaker, 1200 health, with a shovel drop, rows of plots with one gap each, open graves that follow and then lock in, fans of wreaths, and a harder second phase. Both summon Plot Holders, which pay nothing. Beating either floor boss still ends the run. Income: a full clear of the Cemetery pays about 125, or about 140 with every stone blasted. The Records Basement measured about 136 and 152 by the same probe, which also enters its vault and secret room. Estimates from the simulated player, who reads warnings and ground marks but never throws the cane or uses a Thermos: nearly maxed it wins the Records Basement 18 of 20 and the Cemetery 61 of 80, with one level of pills, knees and cane 6 of 20 and 5 of 40, and on a fresh save 3 of 20 and 3 of 40. So the Cemetery runs somewhat harder, which suits a path that opens after a win, but the samples are small. Before tuning, Mortimer's graves were larger and closer together and he won far more often. A vault's Thermos used to lie on the small vault's south door cell and was taken unseen on arrival, now fixed. Still to judge in play: whether the signs get noticed, whether two skulls warn enough, how a Plot Holder's mound and warning read in a crowd, whether rows, furrows and open graves feel fair at speed, whether the many gravestones crowd a fight, whether the Undertaker feels equal to the Reaper, and every placeholder drawing. Long item names on the small vault's and treasure room's item row still touch, as they did before this stage.
- Second floor step 2, Sunset Shores Eternal Care, checked with tests, the full-loop check and screenshots in real windows, not yet played by a person: the open second-floor path is now its own floor. It is a warm, faded care home with pastel walls, woven carpet and linoleum, armchairs and beds (solid, broken only by blasts and bosses), wheelchairs and food trolleys (break like barrels), flower vases (break like grass), potted plants and IV stands. Enemies there have 1.4 times their health and pay 1.5 times, rounded up, and rooms hold 5, 7 and 12 enemies. Some rooms have mopped patches: Grandpa slides a little on them after letting go, a dodge ignores them, charging enemies run on past, and a yellow "Caution: Wet" sign stands beside each patch and can be knocked over. New enemies: the Night Shift Orderly fires one sedative dart down a warned lane, which costs a pill and slows him by half for 1.5 seconds, shown as a pale ring at his feet and drowsy bubbles. The Med-Cart Nurse charges down a warned lane smashing props, and running into a wall stuns her under circling stars, when she takes double damage. The Bingo Caller keeps her distance, calls a number over her head and marks three spots around him (two in a crowded room) that strike 0.9 seconds later. Seasonal Temps and Claims Denial Officers return in the floor's colors. Mini boss Barbara, Activities Coordinator, 500 health, lights a 4 by 4 bingo grid around him that strikes row by row with one safe column, a different one each time, fires a fan of five balls down a warned lane, and wakes two Restless Residents, who pay nothing. Floor boss Matron Mercy, Head of Comfort Care, 1200 health, throws fans of three slowing syringes, charges a gurney 220 px down a lane that stops short of walls and costs two pills, calls Visiting Hours (Orderlies, two at most), and at most every 20 seconds turns the Lights Out: a second of dimming with "Lights out!", then four seconds of 85% darkness but a soft circle around him, during which she only throws syringes. Income: a full clear pays about 217 without blasting and 234 with every prop blasted, against about 136 for the Records Basement, so about 1.6 times rather than 1.5, because every enemy that paid 1 now pays 2. Still to judge in play: whether sliding feels fun or just annoying, whether the slow is long enough to matter or too long, whether Lights Out is fair or should drop to 50% or be replaced, whether the gurney's two pills feel earned by its 0.9 second warning, whether a bingo grid with a safe column reads at speed, whether the many armchairs and beds crowd a fight, the income being a little above target, and every placeholder drawing.
