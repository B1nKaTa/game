# Playtest Notes

## First Version Checklist

- [ ] The full loop from Waiting Room to floor and back plays without crashes.
- [ ] Pension and upgrades survive quitting and reopening the game.
- [ ] A new player usually dies before beating the floor boss.
- [ ] A few rounds of upgrades make a win realistic.
- [ ] A normal run takes about 10 to 15 minutes.
- [ ] The cane combat feels good.

## Session Notes

- Round 1: item naming and moving between rooms feel good. Side room return door moved behind the player. Charge moved to holding attack, and right click now throws the cane. More content is planned.
- Round 2: cane throw and tap-and-hold charge feel better. Fixed a bug where the thrown cane stayed spinning instead of dropping.
- Round 3: added an optometrist selling glasses that show enemy health, and a large boss health bar.
- Round 4: added hit flash, hit stop, screen shake, death bursts, clearer attack warnings, player hurt feedback, flying coins and placeholder sounds.
- Round 5: sounds are fine for now and may change later. Fixed swings passing through enemies: swings and the thrown cane now test the enemy's whole body, a swing stays active while its arc shows, and the arc stays in the direction it was swung.
- Round 6: first real art. Grandpa's idle, walk and swing come from PixelLab. Still to judge in play: the swing's look, and his size next to placeholder enemies. Known gap: the sprite keeps holding the cane while the thrown cane is away.
- Round 7: added a mini map, a big map on M, and return doors. Only visited rooms show. Choosing a route seals the other one, and rooms remember pickups left behind.
- Round 8: Grandpa's collision box moved to his feet. Added Permission Slips, Overheated Thermoses, breakable blocks, loot pockets, locked chests and a locked treasure room. Next rounds: a shop paid in Pension, secret rooms, then special items with mana. Still to judge: drop rates, Thermos strength, and whether rewards make runs too easy.
- Round 9: Slips and Thermoses got their own always-visible HUD counters with icons, and each stacks to 99.
- Round 10: added a shop on every floor and the Retirement Plan. To decide: the real reward list for each tier, and whether a tier should pay once ever. Still to judge: prices, and whether paying in feels worth it.
- Round 11: income was too high. Halved every Pension source, about 124 on a full route instead of 249, so every upgrade takes about 10 clean runs instead of 5. Summons now pay nothing, because a mini boss left alive could be farmed forever. Earning more should come from paid upgrades.
- Next build, not yet run or played: secret rooms, four new combat rooms, a Keys board for rebinding, more Bribed Clerk levels, recipes crafted by Agnes, a crosshair instead of the aim dot, and fading copies of Grandpa behind a dodge.
- Living floor stage 1, checked headless only, not yet seen or played: floors are now rooms on a grid joined by side doors, and everything can be explored and walked back through. Rooms come in small, medium and large, with the camera following in large ones. Both maps are floor plans. Still to judge in play: how the camera feels following in large rooms, whether darkness around small rooms reads well, whether the mini map is readable and too big at 108 by 58, whether bosses that attack from off screen in large rooms stay fair, and how long a full exploration takes. A full clear now passes more combat rooms than the old route, so Pension income goes up until stage 3 and any rebalance.
- Living floor stage 2, checked headless and by looking at screenshots of several rooms, not yet played: rooms now hold rocks (bombs and boss attacks break them, the cane does not), barrels (cane, thrown cane, bombs and bosses break them; most hold nothing or a coin), holes (Grandpa and walking enemies cannot cross, shots, the thrown cane and ghosts can), bone piles, cuttable grass, cobwebs, puddles, floor cracks, torches and wall lanterns. Every room also gets a light scatter from its own seed, and the floor has patches of tone instead of a checkerboard. Both bosses break rocks, barrels and crates their sweep reaches. The big rooms were far too dark away from Grandpa, so rooms now have faint fill lights, enemies carry a soft glow, and dark placeholders are brightened to stay readable. Still to judge in play: whether the fill light makes rooms too flat, how often barrels and rocks block a fight in a way that feels unfair, whether holes read clearly at speed, the coin and potion drop rates, and the placeholder drawings. Small potions heal one pill for now, and the later potion slot should take them instead.
- Living floor stage 3, checked with tests, a simulated player and screenshots in real windows, not yet played by a person: the floor is much harder. Regional Reaper 1200 health, Senior Adjuster 500, regular enemies about 50% tougher and about 50% faster with shorter windups and gaps. Four new enemies: Seasonal Temps (groups of 4 that surround), Notary Publics (aimed bursts, back away), Burnt-Out Auditors (rush in, 1 second fuse, blast hurts other enemies and breaks props) and Claims Denial Officers (slow, frontal shield, the thrown cane goes over it). Rooms hold about half again as many enemy cells, large ones most, and later main path rooms add up to two. The Reaper dashes through props, sweeps, fires rings and summons on marked spots, and runs attacks together below half health. The Adjuster sweeps, charges along a lane and summons across Grandpa's path. Bosses only start attacks from inside the view and hurry over when far away, so nothing starts off screen in large rooms. Enemies hold still for 0.6 seconds as a room starts. Income is back near the old target: a full clear paid about 330 before this stage, 514 with every rock and crate blasted, and now pays about 125, or about 140 with them. A coin from a prop is now worth 1, not a treasure's value. Estimates from a simple simulated player in real scenes, 30 runs each, who changes rooms directly and never uses the Thermos or the thrown cane. A good reflex player wins 4 of 30 on a fresh save, reaching the boss in 7, wins 11 of 30 with one level of pills, knees and cane, and 22 of 30 nearly maxed. On the old numbers the same player won 14 of 20 fresh and 20 of 20 maxed. A slower player wins 0 of 30 fresh and only 1 of 30 nearly maxed, mostly stopped by the Adjuster, which may mean the slow end is too hard, so watch that first. Boss fight time is about 28 to 40 seconds and the Adjuster about 11 to 13, against about 10 and 5 before. Still to judge in play: whether the target really holds for a person, whether the Adjuster is a wall, whether warnings read in a crowd, whether a shop item at 60 now costs too much of a run's income, whether the slower income makes upgrades feel slow, how long a run takes, the block sound, and every placeholder drawing.
- Living floor stage 4, checked with tests, the full-loop check and screenshots in real windows, not yet played by a person: two merchants appear once earned, counted in floors cleared (saved as an optional field so older saves load as zero). Madame Murk, Discount Apothecary, opens after 1 clear and sells brews for the next run, one at a time: Swift Tea 12, Liniment of Fury 16, Healing Tonic 20. Boom Bernard, Fuel Dealer, opens after 3 clears and sells Questionable Fuel, 3 levels at 50, 110 and 220, each making a Thermos blast hurt enemies 30% more and never Grandpa, who still loses 1 pill. The blast gets a ring and a hotter color per level. H drinks the potion in the new slot beside the Thermos counter, which always shows. Barrel small potions now fill the slot (1 pill), and stay on the floor while the slot is full. Reinforced stone is a steel blue block with glowing seams in the wall cell where a secret crack would be. Only a Thermos at the last fuel level breaks it, not Auditor blasts, bosses or the cane. Behind it is a vault: a choice of three items, treasures, and a Small Potion, a Thermos and a Slip in the open. Floors are validated so nothing needed sits behind a vault or a secret wall. Prices were chosen against about 125 Pension a full clear: a brew never costs over a fifth of it, and all permanent upgrades together now cost 2435 instead of 2055. Income is unchanged. Still to judge in play: whether brews at 12 to 20 feel worth a run's Pension, whether the buffs are long and strong enough to matter, whether the unlock points of 1 and 3 clears feel right with one floor, whether a player notices a new merchant has appeared (nothing announces it), whether the stone draws the eye and the resist message is clear, whether vault rewards make the last fuel level worth 380, and the placeholder drawings (the stone, the flask, the merchants are plain colors).
